Three reviewers evaluated the manuscript, and they converge on a set of
substantive concerns regarding novelty, rigor, and presentation, which
are summarized below:

1. Novelty and positioning of the results — Reviewer 5; Reviewer 4. The
main results appear to be straightforward extensions of well-known
results. In particular, Reviewer 5 notes that the link between
vertex-disjoint paths and the rank of associated matrices (Theorem 2)
is already established in reference [3] of the manuscript. Reviewer 4
similarly observes that Theorem 2 "seems like a correct generalization"
of a result in [17] but that the extension is not shown to be
nontrivial, and asks whether Theorem 3 is new. The paper does not
clearly delineate what is original versus what is a restatement of
existing work, which must be clarified.

2. Missing proofs or references for the main theorems — Reviewer 3 ;
Reviewer 4. Theorems 1–3 are given without formal proofs or precise
references. Reviewer 3 notes that the informativity results are
presented only informally as generalizations of prior work, and
recommends inserting formal proofs stating exactly which assumptions
are necessary. Reviewer 4 points out that Theorem 1 is loosely stated
(the spectrum should be positive definite "for almost all \omega"), and
that references on the "transfer of excitation" problem (e.g., Gevers,
Bazanella, Bombois, Miskovic, IEEE TAC, Vol 54, No 12) should be
provided for context.

3. Conceptual clarity and terminology — Reviewer 4; Reviewer 5;
Reviewer 3. The manuscript mixes the notions of identifiability,
network identifiability, generic/local/global identifiability without
clear distinction. Reviewer 4 notes that the definition offered in the
introduction conflates classical identifiability with network
identifiability, and that the claim that partial measurement was not
previously considered is incorrect. Reviewer 5 stresses that
informativity and identifiability are distinct concepts that deserve
separate treatment, and flags that Example 3 appears to contradict a
conjecture discussed in [19], which should be carefully verified.

4. Model formulation and assumptions — Reviewer 5; Reviewer 3; Reviewer
5. The motivation for the model P(q)wk =Q(q)uk , zk =R(q)wk +S(q)uk is
unclear: Reviewer 5 asks why two matrices P(q) and Q(q) are needed and
notes the unusual separation of state and output dynamics compared to
standard network models. Reviewer 3 points out that invertibility of P
is assumed without stating conditions or references, that it is not
stated whether the network topology is known, and that it is unclear
how to select which predictors to use. Reviewer 5 further notes that
the function F(X)=Ry P&#8722;1Qr +Syr is written as a function of X,
yet X does not appear in the expression.

5. Presentation and readability — Reviewer 5; Reviewer 4. Several
sections (Section VI on sub-networks, and the numerical examples in
Sections VII–VIII) are perceived as adding little and could be removed
or substantially condensed. The paper relies on concepts from algebraic
geometry (affine varieties, ideals, fibers, Grobner bases) that are not
standard in the controls community and should be clearly introduced.
The symbol z is used for two different quantities, and there are
numerous typos (e.g., publication year "2913" in reference [21]).

Based on the collective reviews and this editor’s own reading, this
editor finds that the manuscript addresses a relevant topic and the
reviewers acknowledge potential value in some ideas. However, the
manuscript in its current form lacks formal justification for its main
theorems, does not adequately position its novelty relative to the
established literature, and contains unresolved conceptual and modeling
ambiguities. These concerns go beyond minor revision, as the reviewers
question whether the core results are nontrivial extensions. Based on
these grounds, this editor suggest to reject this manuscript but with
the option that it could be resubmitted as a technical note after major
revision. If the authors decide to resubmit, the authors need to
address all concerns raised by the reviewers in the revision, and
moreover, the authors are reminded that IEEE TAC has the policy that
the manuscript will be rejected if it cannot be accepted with minor
revision in the next round of review.
