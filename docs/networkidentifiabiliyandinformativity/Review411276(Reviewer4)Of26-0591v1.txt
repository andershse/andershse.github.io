The paper treats the identification of networks, particularly the
problem of their identifiability,
which is an important topic and has been receiving particular attention
from the community
in the past 15 years or so. It brings new and relevant ideas to the
field and the results seems
correct. Yet, there are various issues to be resolved before
publication in these Transactions
can be considered. 

In the introduction the concepts of informativity and identifiability
are briefly presented. 
These are rather confused, as “identifiability” is a classical concept
in the identification literature
that usually refers to parametrizations of transfer functions, whereas
the
definition offered in the introduction (“whether the network topology
is such that the open loop transfer
functions can be uniquely determined from the predictor.”) looks more
like what is called “network identifiability”
in the literature of network identification. In the introduction itself
the authors “correct” themselves in this regard when,
in the last paragraph, they talk about local network identifiability
instead. Please clarify. 

It is also said that more general networks are considered than in
previous works, which is correct. 
But It is also said “we will consider the partial measurement case …”
implying that previous
works all considered the full measurement case, which is far from the
truth. Please correct. 

Regarding informativity, what is studied is if the input signals are
rich enough to identify the “closed-loop”
relationship from u to z. So, this is standard MIMO identification,
there is nothing “networkly” here, is it?
Then definition 1 seems the standard definition in the literature, so
some reference to it would be welcome;
if this is not the standard definition, references are still needed,
and the difference(s) should be commented.
By the way, this is also the case in other parts of the paper, where
some known results or definitions are 
stated without references; this too must be improved. 

One should not use ‘z’ to denote two different things.

Now, the main issue: the Theorems. Theorems 1 to 3 are given without
proof or reference!

In Theorem 1 the statement itself is loose: it should probably read
“the spectrum is positive
definite for almost all omega …”. With this statement the Theorem is
probably true, but it remains to
be proven. Also, references on this “transfer of excitation” problem
should be given for context and comparison,
so that we (the readers) can tell whether the result is new; for that,
I suggest that the authors look at
the following paper from these Transactions and some of the references
therein: 
Gevers, Bazanella, Bombois, Miskovic - Identification and the
information matrix: how to get just sufficiently rich? 
Vol 54, No 12.

Theorem 2 indeed seems like a correct generalization of the very nice
result in [17], but the extension
is not trivial. Also, more context is welcome here, as similar disjoint
paths conditions have been extensively used
in the field. Theorem 3 also needs a proof and/or references to provide
better context. Is this Theorem new?

Overall, the proofs of the Theorems -  or references to the proofs if
they are not original - must be given and
more reference in the text to the papers in the reference list (which
may be amended a little) must be used
to provide better context.
