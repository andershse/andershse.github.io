This paper presents an extension of well-known results on
identifiability and informativity to a more general networked model.
This reviewer has several comments:

1.- The motivation of this new model is unclear. The authors propose a
model of the form
$P(q)w_k=Q(q)u_k$
$Z_k=R(q)w_k+S(q)u_k$.
It is not justified why there are two matrices P(q) and Q(q) instead of
a single matrix. In addition, it is unusual that the dynamics for the
state and the output are different. Typically, the models in network
identification are of the form:
$w_k=G(q)w_k+Br_k+v_k$
$y_k=Cw_k$,
Natural extensions could involve considering matrices of transfer
functions in B and/or C. However, a comparison or justification of the
proposed model is not provided.

2.- The main results appear to be just straightforward extensions of
well-known results in informativity and identifiability of linear
networks. That is why there are no mathematical proofs, except for
Theorem 4, which is a bit trivial.

3.- Theorem 2 related to the link of vertex-disjoint paths with the
rank of associated matrices is already well-known in identifiability of
linear networks from the reference [3] of the manuscript.

4.- Informativity and identifiability are usually two different
concepts as it has been stated in the reference [13] of the manuscript.
Analyzing both subjects simultaneously does not seem natural and I
think they deserve separate treatment as is usually done.

5.- Several sections of the paper do not provide new information and
give the impression that they could be easily removed. There is a full
page of only examples corresponding to Section VII. In addition,
Section VIII Numerical Examples does not provide interesting
information. Even some of the comments are rather vague like  we can
compute  in 11 minutes on a laptop computer .

6.- The Section VI Sub-networks could be easily removed by analyzing
from the beginning the case of sub-networks where the full network case
can be easily encompassed.

7.- The authors seem to mix notions of identifiability, generic
identifiability, local identifiability, global identifiability, etc.
That is why in Example 3, they seem to contradict the conjecture in the
reference [19] of the manuscript. The authors should verify this
because according to the authors in [19], the conjecture has already
been proven up to five unknown transfer functions in [19]. Also, in
Example 3, the authors claim that there is always another pair of
matrices that satisfy the identifiability. Does this imply just that
the network is unidentifiable?	

8.- The authors introduce the function F of the form
$F(X)=R_yP^{-1}Q_r+S_{yr}$.
This should be a function of $X$, but it does not appear in the
function. What is $X$?

9.- In Example 1, the authors mention that they do not have  global 
network identifiability because there is one solution only when $G_c^1
\neq 0$. By definition, this case $G_c^1 \neq 0$ is excluded since it
implies that there is no edge or connection.

10.- The authors use concepts like affine varieties, ideals, fibers,
Grobner basis, etc. that are not standard in the control systems
community and for this reason, these concepts should be clearly
introduced in the manuscript to improve readability.

11.- There are many typos in the paper like the publication year (2913)
in the reference [21].
