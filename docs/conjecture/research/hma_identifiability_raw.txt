In a determinant term, each selected input has one outgoing
edge and each selected output has one incoming edge. Each
state vertex has at most one edge of either kind, so the term
contains k disjoint input-to-output paths; its other edges form
cycles or diagonal factors. An entry of S gives a one-edge
path. Conversely, k disjoint paths select a determinant term by
using diagonal entries of P at unused state vertices. Distinct
selections give distinct monomials because the structurally
nonzero entries are independent; hence this term cannot cancel
identically. The equivalence holds for every k.

matrices. To count the networks compatible with a given Gc,
we make the dependence on X explicit and define the closed-

F(X) = Ry(X)P(X)−1
Qr(X) + Syr(X).
We keep λ symbolic and regard F as a rational map over K =
C(λ). Known rational transfer functions enter as coefficients.
For the algebraic analysis, we work over an algebraic closure
of K and call sets defined by polynomial equations varieties.
Their dimension is the maximum number of algebraically
independent coordinates. A variety is irreducible if it cannot
be written as the union of two smaller varieties. Let Vo be the
subset of k-tuples on which P is invertible and all expressions
defining F are well-defined. Rational realizations satisfying
these algebraic conditions correspond to points of Vo whose
coordinates belong to K. For these points, equality under F
means equality of rational functions, not equality at a fixed
complex argument. Let Vc = F(Vo) be the image of F. It need
not itself be a variety. We use dim(Vc) to denote the dimension
of its Zariski closure, the smallest variety containing Vc. Here
and below, a property holds generically if it holds outside
a proper algebraic subset of the relevant irreducible variety.
We assume that Vo is irreducible. This holds when the k
free entries have no relations beyond the exclusions used to
define Vo. The closure may contain points with no admissible
preimage; the fiber criterion concerns generic points of the
actual image Vc.

Remark 6: A zero-dimensional fiber consists of finitely
many isolated algebraic solutions. Thus, generic local network
identifiability allows a finite ambiguity, whereas generic net-
work identifiability requires uniqueness. It gives no uniqueness
guarantee for a specified realization, including one on an
exceptional algebraic subset. In particular, there are only
finitely many admissible solutions whose open-loop transfer
functions are rational. The algebraic solutions need not all have
rational coordinates, and any additional requirements such as
properness or stability must also be checked when interpreting
an alternative solution as an admissible network. Legat and
Hendrickx [14] formulate local identifiability using complex
values of transfer functions at a single complex argument
λ and Euclidean neighborhoods. Here, we keep λ symbolic
and use algebraic fibers, without requiring a norm on transfer
functions.

network identifiability in the algebraic sense is equivalent, in

differential is restricted to directions allowed by the constraints
at a generic smooth point and must have rank dim(Vo). Here,
we focus on a Gröbner basis formulation.
