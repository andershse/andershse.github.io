Revision notes for the supplied referee report
=============================================
Manuscript: Generic Local Identifiability Does Not Imply Generic Global
Identifiability in Linear Networks
Author: Anders Hansson
Date: October 8, 2026
Current PDF: local_global_identifiability_submission.pdf

The changes below follow the report's numbered concerns. The main theorem
and its independent-parameter setting are retained. All added identities
and numerical certificates were checked with exact arithmetic. The paper
remains six pages in the official IEEE Control Systems Letters format.

3. Separate the notions of genericity
------------------------------------
The abstract and introduction now state the scalar setting and the scope
of the extension using whole rational transfer functions as coordinates.
The introduction distinguishes this from varying numerator and denominator
coefficients at a prescribed dynamic order and identifies the strictly
proper case as a separate question. Section II defines scalar genericity
through zero-volume exceptional sets. Section VI-A defines algebraic
fibers, the algebraic closure, and algebraic genericity; Section VI-B
separately explains the density of admissible constant witnesses.
The positive-order coefficient-family limitation is repeated in VI-B.

4. Failure of generic global identifiability versus generic ambiguity
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Section II explicitly states that an open ambiguity region disproves
generic global identifiability without asserting ambiguity almost
everywhere. Theorem 1 keeps its original precise quantifiers.
Section V-B now proves that, for every allowed epsilon, open regions with
exactly one spectrally admissible solution also exist. The proof starts
with t < epsilon and zero added couplings, then perturbs all permitted
entries independently. Lemma 1 rules out hidden additional candidates.

The reviewer's exact one-admissible-solution witness was added to the
verification script and archive. With t = 1/2 and added coefficients
scaled by 1/1000, the code verifies:
  ||G1^4||_infinity = 564741/1000000 < 1,
  |trace(G2^3)|/12 = 3999994001/1000000000 > 1.
These certificates and the exact matrices are in the supplied JSON output.
The manuscript includes the general argument; the longer exact witness
is documented in the archive.

5. Consolidate the exceptional domain and identify the involution
----------------------------------------------------------------
Section III introduces one domain, D_b, in equation (8), before stating
the result. It excludes zero x_i, alpha, delta, h_i and the three singular
factors 1-alpha, 1-delta, and 1-alpha*delta. Proposition 1 states that every
point in this domain has exactly two well-posed candidates, both locally
identifiable. It identifies T_b as a rational involution preserving D_b.
The proof supplies explicit transformed h_i and reciprocal eta_i formulas
in equation (15), proves invariance, and identifies the quadratic leading
coefficient as -z_2*z_3*h_1. These new identities are checked symbolically.

6. Explain the augmentation and move the arbitrary-candidate argument
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Section IV now explains the purpose of the added excited and measured
nodes. New Figure 2 shows the two groups, their connections, and inputs
and measurements. Lemma 1 includes the arbitrary-candidate invertibility
argument: an invertible measured transfer matrix forces X to be invertible,
and its inverse's upper-left block forces both internal resolvents to be
invertible. The lemma therefore preserves the exact two-point fiber.
This argument has been removed from the rational-function section.

Section V explains the design before displaying M(t,x). The base
alternatives meet at t = 1. The choices K_0 and L_0 make two matrices have
zero squares; the identity J_0 = G_b0 + B_b*K_0 - L_0*C_b then produces
the upper triangular representation whose fourth power vanishes.
The proof retains the explicit matrices and nilpotence calculation.

7. Explain the conditioning limitation
--------------------------------------
Section V-B now states ||M(1,1)||_infinity = 4 despite spectral radius zero.
It gives J_z = I_3 - t^2*U and sigma_min(J_z) = |1-t^2|, which tends to
zero at the common limit. The paper states that neither a uniform inverse
sensitivity bound nor a uniform ambiguous neighborhood size is obtained
as epsilon decreases. The norm and Gram-matrix identity are verified in
code. No robustness or noisy-estimation claim has been introduced.

8. Explain why the separable-network theorem does not apply
----------------------------------------------------------
The introduction lists the relevant assumptions: fixed topology,
independent edges, partial excitation and measurement, direct feedthrough,
and the same spectral admissibility bound for both candidates.
Section III explicitly contrasts the unknown within-part matrices A,D
with the known internal matrices in the cited separable-network theorem.
The cited primary source was checked for this assumption.

9. Package the rational-function extension carefully
---------------------------------------------------
Section VI is divided into the algebraic extension, proper constant
networks, and reproducibility/conclusions. Proposition 2 proves the
algebraic two-point-fiber claim and preservation of K-valued coordinates.
The following subsection proves the properness and input-output stability
of the constants, followed by the algebraic density argument. It then
states that preservation of arbitrary proper, stable, prescribed-order
families has not been established. The strictly proper case remains open.
The verification archive retains an optional nonconstant proper base
example, without claiming two spectrally admissible networks for it.
